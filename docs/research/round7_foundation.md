## Independent ideas and primary-source check

The research agent generated two ideas before seeing other agents' proposals: (A) jointly infer dynamic latent player and surface strengths rather than supplying incremental ratings to a classifier; (B) use a synthetic-task-pretrained tabular foundation model as the learning algorithm. The task's initial “dynamic strength / matchup” language was already visible and is recorded as a potential anchor. This is AI-assisted scientific brainstorming, not expert consensus. Criteria were structural novelty relative to earlier rounds, temporal validity, bounded computation and interpretability of negative results. The decision owner is the user, with the coordinating agent implementing the authorized experimental scope.

Located evidence, checked 2026-11-27:

TabPFN supports the broader learned-learning-algorithm idea, but its general tabular benchmarks do not establish tennis accuracy.

## Frozen rows, features and controls

Use two context sizes: the latest **2,048 or 4,096 original Tour training rows** before each forward fold. Select by `(tourney_date, match_id)`, independently of labels and future queries. Earlier seasons only; the latest eligible date is earliest validation tournament date−21 days. Mirroring creates 4,096 or 8,192 context rows. Original frozen development targets and the audited Round 6 transfer inference feature matrix are reused.

The same **64 columns** are used by all learners: transfer48; overall/surface 364-day serve and return rate differences (4); corresponding serve-opportunity log sums (2); all six age-profile fields (6); known best-of-five indicator and its log-rank interaction (2); 90-day form and career H2H rate differences (2). Every difference column ends in `_diff`; other fields are exchange-invariant. This preserves technical evidence without exceeding the foundation model's usual pretraining feature range. No player ID, match result, target score or current target technical statistic is an input.

For each context size compare exactly three learners: frozen TabICLv2, logistic C=0.01, and CatBoost depth4/600 iterations/learning-rate0.03/L2=20. Controls use identical original rows, mirrored rows, columns and uniform weights. The two conventional controls retain total weight equal to original rows, consistent with earlier symmetric classifiers. No C/depth search is added. Foundation probabilities and control probabilities are symmetrized as `[p(x)+1−p(swap(x))]/2`; all thresholds stay 0.5. Caching and tests must establish independence from other query rows before processing a year in chunks.

Only the two foundation candidates additionally have fixed probability mixtures with Round 4 at new-model weights 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75. Six fitted learner/context combinations + six deterministic mixtures + Round 4 yield **13 selection entries**. Prior Round 5 accuracy and probability comparators are reported but excluded from selection.

## Selection and reporting

Pick highest 2020–2021 accuracy, breaking ties by lower log loss and then name, subject to Round 4 loss+0.005. The selected entry must improve 2022 accuracy by at least 0.005 and stay within the same loss guard; otherwise retain Round 4. Freeze a pooled 2020–2022 choice before any new 2023 prediction. A failed gate makes that pooled choice exploratory only. The reused 2022 gate is a practical rule, not independent confirmation.

Preserve every candidate's per-fold scores, original context IDs and hashes, the checkpoint/dependency/feature hashes, frozen choices, final inference models and all 2,588 probabilities. Report against matched controls as well as rich older models. Selection on small contexts can underperform because recent rows omit rare players or conditions; a generic synthetic prior may miss sports structure or temporal drift. A null result remains useful and does not establish that every foundation model fails. No performance target is guaranteed.
